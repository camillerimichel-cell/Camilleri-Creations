From a pub debate to the App Store: how LionTrack began

28 September 2026

LionTrack didn't start with a business plan. It started in the pub.

I coach my daughter's football team — a squad of under-11 girls — and one evening a few of us coaches were sitting around planning the end-of-season awards ceremony. The conversation quickly turned into a friendly debate: who were our top players this season? We all had opinions. What none of us had was the evidence.

We were going on gut feel and half-remembered moments from a dozen wet Sunday mornings. And it struck me how much we were missing. If keeping track of who did what across a season was this hard for the coaches, we were almost certainly overlooking quiet, consistent players who never grabbed the headlines but turned up and delivered every week.

Wouldn't it be great to keep track of the stats in an easy, straightforward way — and settle the debate with something more than a hunch?

That was the first half of the idea. The second half came from thinking about the girls themselves.

Two problems, one app

As coaches, we didn't only want better records. We wanted the players more engaged, keener to push their skills further. Kids that age respond to recognition and to a bit of friendly competition — the same instincts that make games so addictive. So the question became: could I build one app that solved both problems at once?

Something that lets coaches track matchday stats quickly and simply on the sideline, while at the same time getting the kids excited about their own performance through a gamified, badge-based interface. Coaches get the data. Players get the motivation. Everyone wins.

Building it for real

That idea became LionTrack. Live match tracking, squad management, and training attendance for the coaching side. XP, badges, streaks, and a player dashboard for the kids. A read-only parent dashboard so families can follow along. And, because not every player or coach experiences an app the same way, a neurodivergent mode and colour themes built in from the start.

The goal throughout was to keep coaches focused on coaching, not admin. The app should fit in your pocket on the touchline and get out of the way while the game is on, then give you a clear picture of the season when you need it.

Live on iOS

Today LionTrack is live on the App Store. It began as a throwaway question over a pint about which of our girls deserved an award — and turned into a real tool for grassroots coaches who want to develop young players and keep them excited about the game.

If that sounds like your team, I'd genuinely love to hear how it works for you.
