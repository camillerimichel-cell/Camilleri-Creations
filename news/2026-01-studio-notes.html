Studio notes: how a piece of fused glass comes together

20 January 2026

People often ask how a finished piece of fused glass starts life. The short answer is: as cold, flat sheets and a rough idea. The longer answer is a slow back-and-forth between planning and letting the kiln have its say.

Cutting and laying up

It begins with choosing colours. Glass behaves differently depending on how compatible the pieces are, so everything in a single work has to be from the same family. Once the palette is set, I score and break the sheets, then arrange the shapes on a kiln shelf. This stage is called the lay-up, and it's the most forgiving — nothing is permanent until the heat does its work.

The firing

Fusing is really about controlling time and temperature. The kiln climbs slowly, holds at the fusing point long enough for the layers to flow into one another, then cools in careful stages so the glass doesn't crack under its own stress. A full cycle can run the better part of a day, and opening the kiln the next morning always carries a little suspense.

Finishing

Out of the kiln, edges are ground smooth and pieces are cleaned. Some go back in for a second firing to slump into a mould — that's how a flat disc becomes a shallow dish. Only after all that does a piece earn a spot in a drop.

That's the process in brief. If you're curious about a specific piece or the techniques behind it, I'm always happy to talk — reach out from the gallery and I'll answer as best I can.
